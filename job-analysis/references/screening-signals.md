# Screening Signals For PM And UX Roles

Use this reference when analyzing internet industry product and design JDs.

## Product manager signals

Look for these groups of evidence:

### Ownership and scope
- Owned a feature, funnel, product area, or roadmap rather than only supporting execution
- Worked across research, design, engineering, operations, and data
- Defined goals, requirements, priorities, or rollout plans

### Business judgment
- Connected product work to growth, conversion, retention, revenue, cost, or efficiency
- Showed tradeoff thinking, prioritization, or strategic framing
- Demonstrated understanding of users, market, or business model

### Execution quality
- Shipped features or projects end to end
- Managed ambiguity and cross-functional alignment
- Used data, experiments, or user feedback to refine decisions

### Strong resume evidence
- Action verbs with ownership
- Quantified outcomes
- Named product area, user group, and business impact
- Clear before/after or problem/solution/result structure

## UX, interaction, and product design signals

Look for these groups of evidence:

### Problem framing
- Clarified user pain points, use cases, or journey issues
- Connected design work to product goals rather than visuals alone

### Process maturity
- Conducted or used research, testing, synthesis, prototyping, and iteration
- Collaborated with PMs, engineers, researchers, or business teams

### System thinking
- Worked on flows, service experience, information architecture, or design systems
- Balanced usability, feasibility, and business constraints

### Strong resume evidence
- Case bullets with context, action, and result
- Metrics such as conversion, task completion, satisfaction, or efficiency
- Evidence of shipped work, not only concept work

## HR initial-screen priorities

In early screening, HR often checks:
- Title and function match
- Industry and domain match
- Years and seniority match
- Recognizable scope or company context
- Tool and keyword overlap
- Whether bullets are readable and outcome-oriented

If a resume lacks obvious alignment, HR may reject before a hiring manager reviews details.

## Common hidden expectations

Infer these carefully:
- "Cross-functional" often means stakeholder management and ambiguity handling
- "Data-driven" often means metric ownership, experiments, or decision support with data
- "User-centered" often means research-informed decisions and evidence of iteration
- "Strategy" often means prioritization and business understanding, not just long documents
- "0 to 1" often means ambiguity tolerance, fast learning, and shipping in uncertain conditions
